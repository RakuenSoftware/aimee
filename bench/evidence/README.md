Everything here is generated from or quoted directly out of the artefacts in

that cannot be re-derived.

| file | what it is |
| --- | --- |

## The through-line

The headline result of this exercise is how many times the measurement was wrong, and in which
direction. Which model won is the smaller finding.

Twenty-three defects are recorded, and the expensive ones share a shape: the
harness applied a constraint the product does not, and the resulting number
looked like a statement about the model.

- A **confidence floor** the product had already removed took Qwen3-0.6B from
  0.4058 to 0.0000 and produced the claim that nothing under 600M parameters
  works. The prompt's own schema example contains `"confidence":0.0` and small
  models copy it. (defect 17)
- A **token cap** at a quarter of production's produced a ladder where 12B
  bought 0.002 over E4B and where thinking appeared to hurt larger models. Both
  were the cap truncating exactly the models that reason longest, and the empty
  rows counted twice: as abstentions and as missed facts. (defect 23)
- The same **refusal-to-score-a-blocked-row** check had to be learned four
  times, in two files, for three different causes: `--max-tokens`, `--timeout`,
  and then `--max-tokens` again in the other tier. (defects 16, 18, 23)
- A directory named **`gpu`** held models llama.cpp had quietly placed on CPU,
  because they did not fit and auto-fit said nothing. (defect 15)
- Two copies of one sweep ran **against the same port** because a `pkill` was
  issued and never checked. (defect 22)

Every one of these produced a plausible number. None of them produced an error.
That is the point worth writing about: a benchmark does not fail loudly, it
fails with four decimal places.
