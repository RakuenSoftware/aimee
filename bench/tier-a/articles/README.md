# Tier-A extraction benchmark: article series

Five drafts from one benchmarking effort. The task throughout is the same: read a
short note, return subject-relation-object triples as JSON, or return an empty
list if the note asserts nothing durable. Models are local and small, from 230M
to 4.5B effective parameters.

| # | article | what it argues |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [How small can a fact extractor be](01-how-small-can-an-extractor-be.md) | Usable extraction starts around 800M to 1B, and the two ways small models fail need separate scores |
| 2 | [The confidence interval said no](02-the-confidence-interval-said-no.md) | Replication across corpora settles small effects that a per-run interval never will |
| 3 | [Repeatable, not identical](03-repeatable-not-identical.md) | A benchmark needs a configuration that reproduces itself, not one that matches sequential |
| 4 | [Fast because it was broken](04-fast-because-it-was-broken.md) | Four benchmark defects, none of which threw an error and all of which produced believable numbers |
| 5 | [More correct than production](05-the-benchmark-was-more-correct-than-production.md) | The scorer is a specification, and diffing it against the pipeline found four production bugs |

All five are drafts. Evidence and working notes are in

## Caveats that apply to the whole series

State these once here rather than five times.

**The corpus cannot be regenerated.** The generator is seeded and deterministic;
its inventory inputs were never tracked in git, and zero of 1001 notes reproduce
from the surviving files. Corpus v5 was derived from v4 by relabelling, with
stable ids and note text, so predictions can be re-scored across the two. Nothing
can be rebuilt from source. Article 5 covers this.

**Configurations are not interchangeable across arms.** Multi-token prediction
moves 26 of 100 notes relative to a sequential run; 32 parallel slots move 25 of
100 relative to *themselves*; and running the identical arm on one server rather
than three moves 356 of 1001 notes and 0.0024 strict F1. Any comparison in this
series is valid only within one configuration. Article 3 covers this.

**Within one configuration, repetition is exact.** Three independent runs of the
same three-process arm, spread over days across server restarts, returned
byte-identical completions on all 1001 notes in every pairwise comparison, and
the same strict F1 to four decimal places. Run-to-run noise is not a limit on
anything in this series; configuration differences are. Article 3 covers this.

**Article 1's rankings predate the corpus and prompt fixes.** Every number in it
was measured before the reasoning-suppression fix, the ontology expansion and the
`runs_on` template correction. Treat its ordering as a hypothesis. Articles 4 and
5 are the reasons why.

## Hardware

Two cards, and they are not interchangeable in any throughput comparison:

- NVIDIA RTX 5080, 16 GiB, CUDA, in a shared container
- AMD Radeon RX 7900 XTX, 24 GiB, Vulkan (no ROCm tooling on that host)

Where a wall-clock or notes-per-minute figure appears, the card is named. Two
figures measured on different cards are never divided.

## Open measurement items

Consolidated from the five articles, in rough order of what would change a
conclusion.

1. Finish the 10,000-note quant ladder. The three E4B arms are banked (Q4 0.6324,
   Q6 0.6450, Q8 0.6321); the three E2B arms are outstanding (articles 1, 2).
   Q8's regression below Q6 needs a second corpus before it can be stated as a
   finding rather than an observation.
2. Re-run the top models on corpus v5. Article 1's table predates it (article 1).
3. Intervals on relation-agnostic F1. The bootstrap tool scores strict only, so
   the recall figure that carried the thinking decision has none (article 4).
4. Sharded throughput on matched hardware, and where the returns stop
   (article 3).
5. A corpus generated by a different pipeline, to test the independence
   assumption behind the sign test (article 2).
6. Test the prompt clause against the other fourteen models. Two are checked
   (article 4).
7. Line-by-line diff of scorer normalisations against the production pipeline
   (article 5).
